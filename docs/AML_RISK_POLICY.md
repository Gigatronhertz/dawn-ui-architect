## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Karije prevents its services from being used for money laundering (ML), terrorist financing (TF) or the financing of weapons proliferation (PF). It covers every customer, every supplier and every payment that Karije receives or makes. It applies to all directors, staff and contractors.

It is written to meet Karije's obligations under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022, the Terrorism (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 and the requirements of our banking partners.

## 2. Our business and risk profile

Karije is an online travel company. We sell group trips within Nigeria through karije.com. Karije is the merchant of record: travellers buy trips from Karije, pay Karije, and Karije handles cancellations and refunds. Some trips are delivered by partner travel agencies, which Karije contracts as suppliers and pays after the trip is delivered.

We also receive our own revenue from monthly software subscriptions paid by travel agencies (Karije Pro) and from fees paid by consumers for trip-planning tools.

Our overall risk is assessed as low to medium:

- Customers are individuals buying domestic travel, at typical prices of ₦15,000 to ₦250,000 per traveller.
- Payments are received by bank transfer into a virtual account issued for each booking, so every payment is tied to one identified booking.
- Payouts go only to suppliers we have reviewed, into bank accounts verified in their name.
- We do not handle cash, foreign currency, cryptocurrency or cross-border payments.

The main risks we watch for are: unusually large or repeated bookings used to move money; bookings paid by third parties; refunds used to return "cleaned" money to a different account; and suppliers that are not genuine businesses or that redirect payouts.

## 4. Customer due diligence

### 4.1 Travellers (customers)

Before a booking is confirmed we record the traveller's full name, email address and phone number. Each booking is linked to an email address the traveller has verified (by a sign-in link or Google sign-in). Payment is made by bank transfer into a virtual account issued for that booking only.

### 4.2 Supplier travel agencies

Before any supplier's trip is offered for sale, we collect:

- the agency's business name, address and phone number;
- the National Identification Number (NIN) of the person responsible, and an identity document;
- a business document (for example a CAC certificate) where the supplier is a registered business.

A member of our team reviews every application manually. Trips from a supplier are not offered for sale until the supplier is approved. A supplier's payout bank account is checked by account-name enquiry with the bank; we pay only into an account in the supplier's own name.

### 4.3 Subscription and fee customers

Agencies that buy a Karije Pro subscription go through the supplier review in 4.2. Consumers paying planning fees are identified as in 4.1.

## 5. Risk classification

Every customer and supplier is placed in one of three risk levels.

### Low

- Travellers making ordinary bookings within normal price ranges, paid from their own account.
- Verified suppliers with a stable payout account and no flags.

Low-risk relationships receive the standard checks in section 4.

### Medium

- All supplier agencies, because Karije pays money out to them.
- Any customer or supplier with an open medium-level flag (section 7).

Medium-risk relationships receive the standard checks plus review of each flag within 2 business days.

### High

- Any politically exposed person (PEP), their family members or close associates.
- Any customer or supplier with an open high-level flag (section 7).
- Any supplier we have not been able to verify, or whose details do not match their bank account.
- Anyone linked to a country or person on a sanctions list (section 8).

High-risk relationships receive enhanced due diligence (section 6) and need the Compliance Officer's approval before any payout is made to them or on their trip.

### Factors we consider

When placing someone in a level we consider: who they are (individual or business, PEP status, whether their identity was verified); what they are buying and how much; how they pay (from their own account or someone else's); how they behave (frequency of bookings, cancellations and refund requests, changes of payout account); and where they are (any link to sanctioned countries or persons).

## 6. Enhanced due diligence (EDD)

For a high-risk customer or supplier, the Compliance Officer will, as appropriate:

- ask for additional identification and proof of address;
- ask where the money comes from (source of funds);
- confirm a supplier's details by phone using the number on their original application, never one supplied in the same request;
- hold any payout on the affected trip until the review is complete;
- record the decision and reasons.

If EDD cannot be completed, we decline the booking or end the relationship and refund to the original paying account.

## 7. Transaction monitoring

Our system raises the following flags automatically. They are listed in the admin dashboard (Risk tab) until someone reviews them.

| Flag | Raised when | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Large payment | A single payment of ₦500,000 or more | Medium (High at ₦1,000,000 or more) |
| Many places, one contact | Three or more places on one trip booked under the same email or phone number | Medium |
| Many bookings, short time | Three or more bookings under the same email or phone number within 7 days | Medium |
| Payout account changed | A supplier or organiser changes their payout account after customers have paid | High |
| Unverified supplier paid | Customers pay for a trip run by a supplier not yet verified | High |

High flags are reviewed before any payout on the affected trip. Medium flags are reviewed within 2 business days. Every review records an outcome (cleared, escalated, or reported to the NFIU), a note of what was checked, the reviewer's name and the date.

The large-payment threshold is reviewed at least once a year against actual booking values.

## 8. Sanctions screening

Before approving a supplier, and before any payout to a high-risk customer or supplier, the Compliance Officer checks the names against:

- the United Nations Security Council Consolidated List;
- the Nigeria Sanctions List published by the Nigeria Sanctions Committee.

Screening is currently done by hand. Any possible match stops the relationship until it is resolved; a confirmed match is reported as in section 10, and no payment is made.

## 9. Refunds

Refunds are paid only to the bank account the original payment came from. A request to refund to a different account is treated as a high-risk flag and needs the Compliance Officer's approval.

## 11. Record keeping

We keep the following for at least 5 years after the end of the relationship or the date of the transaction:

- identification and due-diligence records for customers and suppliers;
- records of every payment, refund and payout;
- every risk flag and its review;
- copies of any reports made to the NFIU.

Records are kept securely in our systems with access limited to authorised staff.

Everyone who handles customers, suppliers or payments is trained on this policy when they start and at least once a year after that.
